# Exploration Priority Methodology

## Separation from Career Fit

Career Fit answers: how closely the current Profile overlaps with a Career Profile in the 60-career library. Exploration Priority answers: when a user does not know where to begin, which direction is most useful to examine first.

Exploration Priority never changes Career Fit, Base Talent, RIASEC, Confidence, or Career vectors. It produces labels and an ordering, not a new 0–100 score.

## Primary signal

The mean Career Fit of each direction's representative careers is the primary ordering signal. Talent, Interest, Work Style, Environment, and Values are not added again because they are already components of Career Fit.

## Tie-breakers

The following factors are used only when directions are in the same Score Proximity Cluster or when confidence cannot support a unique leader:

- Confidence: prefer evidence supported by medium/high confidence over low confidence.
- Entry Distance: prefer a direction that can be tested or entered with fewer current gaps; it does not lower structural fit.
- Potential Friction: use the number of concrete friction reasons to choose what to verify first.
- Energy Drain Risk: use negative energy signals among shared Talents; ability and energy remain separate.
- Explicit exploration preference: direct tie-breaker answers can move a direction within its proximity cluster only.
- Direction diversity: representative careers are assigned to one direction, preventing one family from filling every primary slot.

No tie-breaker can move a direction ahead of a clearly separated higher Career Fit cluster. Tie-breaker answers are stored as exploration preferences and never enter the assessment or matching pipeline.

## User-facing labels

- `priority`: 優先探索
- `equally_worth`: 同樣值得探索
- `compare`: 可以保留比較
- `not_priority`: 目前不是優先

These labels describe exploration order, not suitability grades.
